Q2Standard Form
Your answer:

Equating real and imaginary parts of $a+2b+2ai = 4+6i$:

$2a = 6 \Rightarrow a=3$

$a+2b=4 \Rightarrow 3+2b=4 \Rightarrow b=\dfrac{1}{2}$

Answer: $a=3,\ b=\dfrac12$

Q3Conjugate
Your answer:

$\sqrt{-5}-\sqrt7\,i = \sqrt5\,i-\sqrt7\,i$ (writing $\sqrt{-5}$ as $\sqrt5\,i$)

Conjugate flips the sign of the imaginary part only.

Answer: conjugate is $\sqrt5\,i+\sqrt7\,i$, i.e. $\sqrt{-5}+\sqrt7\,i$

Q4Prove That — Powers of i
Your answer:

$(1+i)^2 = 1+2i+i^2 = 2i$ (since $i^2=-1$), so $(1+i)^4 = (2i)^2 = -4$

$1+\dfrac1i = 1+\dfrac{1}{i}\cdot\dfrac{i}{i} = 1-i$ (since $\dfrac1i=-i$)

$(1-i)^2 = -2i \Rightarrow (1-i)^4 = (-2i)^2 = -4$

Product: $(-4)\times(-4) = 16$   Hence proved.

Q5Square Root of Complex Number
Your answer:

Let $\sqrt{-8-6i}=a+bi$. Squaring: $-8-6i = (a^2-b^2)+2abi$

So $a^2-b^2=-8$ and $2ab=-6 \Rightarrow ab=-3$

Using $a^2+b^2=\sqrt{(a^2-b^2)^2+(2ab)^2}=\sqrt{64+36}=10$

$a^2-b^2=-8,\ a^2+b^2=10 \Rightarrow a^2=1,\ b^2=9 \Rightarrow a=\pm1,\ b=\mp3$ (signs matched via $ab=-3$)

Answer: $\pm(1-3i)$

Q7Modulus

Rationalise: $\dfrac{2-i}{1+i}\cdot\dfrac{1-i}{1-i} = \dfrac{(2-i)(1-i)}{2} = \dfrac{2-2i-i+i^2}{2}=\dfrac{1-3i}{2}$

$|z| = \dfrac{\sqrt{1^2+3^2}}{2} = \dfrac{\sqrt{10}}{2}$

Q8Argument
Your answer:

$z=-1-i$ lies in the third quadrant. Reference angle: $\tan^{-1}\left|\dfrac{-1}{-1}\right| = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$

Third quadrant argument $= -\left(\pi-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right) = -\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$

Answer: $\arg(z) = -\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$

Q13Prove That — Cube Roots of Unity
Your answer:

Using $1+\omega+\omega^2=0 \Rightarrow 1-\omega^2 = 2\omega$... instead directly: $1+\omega-\omega^2 = (1+\omega+\omega^2)-2\omega^2 = -2\omega^2$

So $(1+\omega-\omega^2)^6 = (-2\omega^2)^6 = 64\cdot\omega^{12} = 64\cdot(\omega^3)^4 = 64\cdot1 = 64$

Hence proved.

Q14Prove That — Cube Roots, General
Your answer:

Expand $(a+b\omega+c\omega^2)(a+b\omega^2+c\omega)$ term by term, then substitute $\omega^3=1$ and $1+\omega+\omega^2=0$ wherever $\omega^3, \omega^4$ appear.

After collecting terms: $a^2+b^2\omega^3+c^2\omega^3 + ab(\omega^2+\omega) + bc(\omega^4+\omega^2) + ac(\omega+\omega^2)$

Since $\omega^3=1$ and $\omega+\omega^2=-1$: this simplifies to $a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-bc-ca$

Hence proved.

Q15Prove That — Binomial + i
Your answer:

$(a+ib)^3 = a^3+3a^2(ib)+3a(ib)^2+(ib)^3 = a^3+3a^2bi-3ab^2-b^3i$

$= (a^3-3ab^2) + (3a^2b-b^3)i$

Equate: $x=a^3-3ab^2,\ \ y=3a^2b-b^3$

$\dfrac{x}{a}=a^2-3b^2,\ \ \dfrac{y}{b}=3a^2-b^2$

Sum: $4a^2-4b^2 = 4(a^2-b^2)$   Hence proved.
