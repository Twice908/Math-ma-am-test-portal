A1Definition
Your answer:

A sequence is a function whose domain is the set of natural numbers; each output is a term $t_n$.

A series is the sum of the terms of a sequence: $S_n = t_1 + t_2 + \cdots + t_n$

General term of A.P.: $t_n = a + (n-1)d$, where $a$ is the first term and $d$ is the common difference.

A2A.P. — nth Term
Your answer:

Given: $a = 5$, $d = 4$, $n = 12$

$t_{12} = 5 + (11)(4) = 5 + 44 = 49$

Answer: 49

A3A.P. — Sum Formula
Your answer:

Given: $a = 4$, $d = 5$, $n = 30$

$S_{30} = \dfrac{30}{2}[2(4) + 29(5)] = 15[8+145] = 15 \times 153 = 2295$

Answer: 2295

A4A.P. — Finding $a$ and $d$
Your answer:

$t_7 = a + 6d = 34$ ... (1), $t_{13} = a + 12d = 64$ ... (2)

Subtract (1) from (2): $6d = 30 \Rightarrow d = 5$

From (1): $a = 34 - 30 = 4$

Answer: $a = 4$, $d = 5$

A5A.P. — Solving for $n$
Your answer:

$S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}[2(2) + (n-1)(3)] = 155$

$\dfrac{n}{2}[4 + 3n - 3] = 155 \Rightarrow n(3n + 1) = 310$

$3n^2 + n - 310 = 0 \Rightarrow n = \dfrac{-1 + 61}{6} = 10$

Answer: $n = 10$

D2A.G.P. — Sum Formula
Your answer:

Let $S = 1 + 3x + 5x^2 + \cdots + (2n-1)x^{n-1}$

$xS = x + 3x^2 + 5x^3 + \cdots + (2n-1)x^n$

$(1-x)S = 1 + 2(x + x^2 + \cdots + x^{n-1}) - (2n-1)x^n$

Answer: $S_n = \dfrac{1 + 2x(1-x^{n-1})/(1-x) - (2n-1)x^n}{1-x}$

E4Special Series
Your answer:

E5General Term Method
Your answer:

$S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n (k^2 + 2k) = \dfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} + n(n+1) = \dfrac{n(n+1)(2n+7)}{6}$

$S_{10} = \dfrac{10 \times 11 \times 27}{6} = 495$

Answer: $S_n = \dfrac{n(n+1)(2n+7)}{6}$; $S_{10} = 495$

F1Prove That — G.P. Property
Your answer:

Let $q = pk$, $r = pk^2$, $s = pk^3$

$(p+q) = p(1+k)$, $(q+r) = pk(1+k)$, $(r+s) = pk^2(1+k)$

Common ratio: $k$. Hence $(p+q), (q+r), (r+s)$ are in G.P.

F5Series from S(n)

$t_n = S_n - S_{n-1} = (3n^2 + 2n) - (3(n-1)^2 + 2(n-1)) = 6n - 1$
