Section BVery Short Answers

B1. An equation is dimensionally correct only if the dimensions of every term on both sides, including terms being added or subtracted, are identical.

B2. If two vectors are represented in magnitude and direction by two sides of a triangle taken in order, their resultant is represented by the third side taken in the opposite order.

B3. Relative velocity is the velocity of one object with respect to another: $\mathbf v_{AB}=\mathbf v_A-\mathbf v_B$.

B4. Impulse is the product of force and the time for which it acts, $J=F\Delta t$. It equals the change in momentum, $\Delta p$.

C2Dimensional Analysis

$[v^2]=[u^2]=[LT^{-1}]^2=[L^2T^{-2}]$ and $[2as]=[LT^{-2}][L]=[L^2T^{-2}]$.

All three terms have identical dimensions, so the equation is dimensionally consistent.

C3Vector Addition

$R=\sqrt{3^2+4^2+2(3)(4)\cos90^\circ}=\sqrt{25}=5$ units.

$\tan\phi=\dfrac{4\sin90^\circ}{3+4\cos90^\circ}=\dfrac43$, hence $\phi\approx53.1^\circ$ from the 3-unit vector.

C4Scalar Product

$\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B=|\mathbf A||\mathbf B|\cos\theta$ is a scalar equal to one vector times the component of the other along it. For example, work is $W=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf d=Fd\cos\theta$.

C5Projectile Motion

Vertical fall: $20=\frac12(10)t^2$, so $t=2\,\text{s}$. Horizontal distance: $x=ut=10\times2=20\,\text{m}$.

C6Circular Motion

$\mathbf r=r(\cos\omega t\,\hat i+\sin\omega t\,\hat j)$, so $\mathbf v=\dfrac{d\mathbf r}{dt}$ and $\mathbf a=\dfrac{d\mathbf v}{dt}=-\omega^2\mathbf r$.

Therefore, $|\mathbf a|=\omega^2r=\dfrac{v^2}{r}$, directed towards the centre.

C7Newton's Second Law

The rate of change of momentum is proportional to the applied force and is in its direction: $F\propto\dfrac{dp}{dt}=\dfrac{d(mv)}{dt}$.

For constant mass, $F\propto m\dfrac{dv}{dt}=ma$. In SI units, the proportionality constant is 1, hence $F=ma$.

C8Collision

Taking the 2 kg body's direction as positive: $2(4)+3(-2)=5v$, so $v=0.4\,\text{m/s}$, in the initial direction of the 2 kg body.

D1Errors in Measurement

Systematic errors are consistent and one-directional, such as an instrument zero error, calibration defect, personal bias or temperature drift. They are reduced by correction, calibration and improved technique.

Random errors are unpredictable fluctuations, such as small changes in reaction time. They are minimised by taking many readings, finding their mean and applying statistical treatment.

D2Parallelogram Law

If two vectors acting at a point are represented by adjacent sides of a parallelogram, their resultant is represented by the diagonal through that point. For vectors $A$ and $B$ at angle $\theta$, resolve the diagonal into $A+B\cos\theta$ and $B\sin\theta$.

$R^2=(A+B\cos\theta)^2+(B\sin\theta)^2=A^2+B^2+2AB\cos\theta$, hence $R=\sqrt{A^2+B^2+2AB\cos\theta}$.

The direction $\phi$ from $A$ is $\tan\phi=\dfrac{B\sin\theta}{A+B\cos\theta}$.

D3Projectile Motion

$u_x=40\cos30^\circ=20\sqrt3$ and $u_y=40\sin30^\circ=20\,\text{m/s}$.

Maximum height: $H=\dfrac{u_y^2}{2g}=20\,\text{m}$. Time of flight: $T=\dfrac{2u_y}{g}=4\,\text{s}$. Range: $R=u_xT=80\sqrt3\approx138.6\,\text{m}$.

D4Elastic Collision

In the absence of external force, total linear momentum remains constant. Thus $m_1u_1+m_2u_2=m_1v_1+m_2v_2$.

Conservation of kinetic energy gives $m_1(u_1^2-v_1^2)=m_2(v_2^2-u_2^2)$. Dividing by the momentum relation gives $u_1-u_2=v_2-v_1$.

Solving: $v_1=\dfrac{(m_1-m_2)u_1+2m_2u_2}{m_1+m_2}$, $v_2=\dfrac{(m_2-m_1)u_2+2m_1u_1}{m_1+m_2}$.
