Section BVery Short Answers

B1. Atomic number ($Z$) is the number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom. Mass number ($A$) is the total number of protons and neutrons present in the nucleus.

B2. Isotones are atoms of different elements having the same number of neutrons but different atomic numbers. Example: $^{14}_{6}C$ and $^{15}_{7}N$.

B3. Rutherford's model could not explain the stability of an atom. According to classical theory, a revolving electron should continuously lose energy and eventually fall into the nucleus.

B4. An atomic orbital is a region of space around the nucleus where the probability of finding an electron is maximum.

C1Subatomic Particles

Electron: relative charge $-1$, relative mass approximately $1/1837$.

Proton: relative charge $+1$, relative mass approximately $1$.

Neutron: relative charge $0$, relative mass approximately $1$.

C2Atomic Structure

For $^{35}_{17}Cl$: atomic number $Z=17$ and mass number $A=35$.

Protons $=17$.

For a neutral atom, electrons $=17$.

Neutrons $=A-Z=35-17=18$.

C3Isotopes and Isobars

Isotopes: atoms of the same element having the same atomic number but different mass numbers. Example: $^{1}_{1}H$, $^{2}_{1}H$, $^{3}_{1}H$.

Isobars: atoms of different elements having the same mass number but different atomic numbers. Example: $^{40}_{18}Ar$ and $^{40}_{20}Ca$.

C4Rutherford Model

According to Rutherford's model, electrons revolve around the nucleus. A revolving charged particle should continuously radiate energy. Therefore, the electron would lose energy, its orbit would shrink and it would eventually fall into the nucleus. Thus, the model could not explain atomic stability.

C5Bohr Model

Electrons revolve around the nucleus only in certain permitted circular orbits called stationary orbits. Electrons in these orbits do not radiate energy. The angular momentum is quantised: $mvr=\dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$. Energy is emitted or absorbed only when an electron moves from one permitted orbit to another.

C6Bohr Energy

For the first orbit, $n=1$.

$E_1=-\dfrac{2.18\times10^{-18}}{1^2}$

$E_1=-2.18\times10^{-18}\,J$

C7de Broglie Relationship

de Broglie's relationship is $\lambda=\dfrac{h}{mv}=\dfrac{h}{p}$.

Here, $\lambda$ is the wavelength associated with the moving particle, $h$ is Planck's constant, $m$ is the mass of the particle, $v$ is its velocity and $p$ is its momentum.

C8Quantum Numbers

For a 3p orbital:

$n=3$.

For a p-subshell, $l=1$.

$m_l=-1,0,+1$.

D1Subatomic Particles

Electron: J. J. Thomson discovered the electron through cathode ray experiments and established that cathode rays consist of negatively charged particles.

Proton: Positive rays were studied by E. Goldstein. The positively charged particles were later identified as protons.

Neutron: James Chadwick discovered the neutron in 1932 through experiments involving the bombardment of beryllium with alpha particles.

D2Bohr Model

Bohr proposed that electrons revolve around the nucleus only in certain permitted stationary orbits. Electrons in stationary orbits do not radiate energy. The angular momentum of an electron is quantised according to $mvr=\dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$.

Energy is emitted or absorbed when an electron jumps between two stationary orbits. The energy of an electron in the nth orbit is expressed as $E_n=-\dfrac{2.18\times10^{-18}}{n^2}\,J$ for hydrogen.

D3Quantum Numbers

Principal quantum number ($n$): specifies the main shell and energy level. Allowed values are $n=1,2,3,\ldots$.

Azimuthal quantum number ($l$): specifies the subshell and shape of the orbital. For a given $n$, $l=0$ to $(n-1)$.

Magnetic quantum number ($m_l$): specifies the orientation of an orbital. For a given $l$, $m_l=-l$ to $+l$.

Spin quantum number ($m_s$): specifies the spin of the electron. Its allowed values are $+\dfrac12$ and $-\dfrac12$.

D4Electronic Configuration

Aufbau principle: Electrons are filled in orbitals in order of increasing energy.

Pauli's exclusion principle: An orbital can contain a maximum of two electrons and they must have opposite spins.

Hund's rule: Electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly with parallel spins before pairing.

Nitrogen (Z = 7): $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^3$ → $1s(\uparrow\downarrow)\;2s(\uparrow\downarrow)\;2p(\uparrow)(\uparrow)(\uparrow)$.

Oxygen (Z = 8): $1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^4$ → $1s(\uparrow\downarrow)\;2s(\uparrow\downarrow)\;2p(\uparrow\downarrow)(\uparrow)(\uparrow)$.
